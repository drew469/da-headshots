How good would Antonio Brown have been with no off-field issues? I simulated his career one hundred thousand times, on his best behavior.
Through age thirty, he had eleven thousand two hundred seven receiving yards, and six straight seasons with a hundred catches. Nobody else has ever done that.
Then it came apart. A trade out of Pittsburgh. Released by the Raiders before he played a game. One game in New England. Two suspensions. And in January twenty twenty-two, he walked off the field mid-game against the Jets. He never played again.
So I found the thirty-four receivers since nineteen ninety-nine who looked most like him at thirty. Julio Jones, Andre Johnson, Calvin Johnson and Larry Fitzgerald. Every simulated career follows one of their real paths, adjusted to his level.
Is it honest? I predicted each of those thirty-four from the others. Seventeen landed in the middle half of the forecast. Right on target. If you want more player breakdowns like this, follow Daily Athlete.
The typical well-behaved Antonio Brown finishes with about thirteen thousand seven hundred yards and eighty-eight touchdowns. He beats his real career total eighty-six percent of the time.
But the ceiling is wild. In more than one in four careers, he clears fifteen thousand yards. Only five receivers have ever done that. In about one in ten, he passes Larry Fitzgerald for second all-time. Jerry Rice? Two careers out of a hundred thousand.
