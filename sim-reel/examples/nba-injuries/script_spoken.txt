NBA players are moving more than ever. In the nineteen eighties, players covered about two hundred eighty-six feet every minute. Today? Three hundred ninety. And the injuries are piling up. Haliburton. Ja. Giannis. Chet. So why is everyone getting hurt?
Here's the strange part. Stars are actually playing less. In nineteen seventy-three, the top players averaged almost forty minutes a game. By the late nineties, about thirty-five. Today? Thirty-two. Fewer minutes, more injuries. So what's going on?
Theory one: specialization. Kids used to play three sports. Now they pick basketball early and play it all year. Same muscles. Same joints. Every single day.
Theory two: sneakers. Almost everyone wears low tops now. Bill Simmons swears that's part of it.
Theory three: size. Centers went from two hundred twelve pounds in nineteen fifty-two to two hundred fifty-seven in twenty fourteen. Guards, from one seventy-eight to two oh one. More weight means more force on every landing.
So how do you fix it? Option one: cut games from forty-eight minutes to forty. Option two: cut the season from eighty-two games to sixty or sixty-five. Both mean less money for the league. Option three: let defenders hand-check again, like they do in Europe. Fewer explosive drives, maybe fewer injuries.
Either way, the NBA might have to leave some money on the table to protect its stars. Which fix would you make? Let me know.
